## Defensibility / moat (once the model is proven)
Five factors identified, not yet prioritized/ranked against each other:
1. **Regulatory head start** — being first to solve the EWA-vs-real-payroll legal structure (see [[Legal]]) and secure state licensing/partnerships is slow and hard for competitors to replicate quickly.
2. **Employer + worker network effects** — once workers hold a Puddl3 wallet/card and employers are embedded, switching costs and habitual usage create lock-in on both sides.
3. **Partner/vendor relationships** — deep integration and negotiated terms across the embedded payroll, BaaS, WaaS, and card partners (see [[Tech Stack]]) are hard for a new entrant to replicate quickly.
4. **Brand/trust as category creator** — first-to-market, category-defining positioning ("the instant USDC payroll company," even though the crypto layer is hidden from end users per [[Marketing]]).
5. **Zero employer cost as a structural pricing advantage** — competitors charging employers directly (or charging workers advance fees in the EWA model) have a harder time matching a model that costs the employer nothing and where workers earn instant access to genuinely final pay. Ties directly to the [[Revenue Models]] structure being the moat, not just a pricing choice.

## Market sizing / TAM (researched)
Figures below are the best available public data as of research date; cited sources noted per line. Treat as directional order-of-magnitude, not a precise addressable-market model — no source breaks out "hourly W-2 workers at employers likely to buy instant-pay software," so these are total industry employment figures, not a bottoms-up SAM.

**National, by target vertical ([[Marketing]] anchor verticals):**
- Restaurant/foodservice industry: **~15.8 million jobs** projected for 2026 (National Restaurant Association, 2026 State of the Restaurant Industry).
- Retail industry: **~15.6 million** direct payroll employees as of March 2025 (BLS, via NRF).
- Staffing/temp industry: **~2 million** temporary and contract workers employed per week (Q4 2025 average); **~11 million** individuals placed by staffing firms over the course of 2024 (American Staffing Association).
- Combined, these three anchor verticals alone represent roughly **30-40+ million workers**, the large majority paid hourly — before counting the "broader SMB hourly workforce" category, which is larger still.
- General context stat: hourly-paid workers have historically made up roughly **55-59% of all U.S. wage and salary workers** (most recent precise breakdown found: 58.5%, ~78.2 million workers, BLS CPS) — current-year exact percentage not confirmed this session, but the order of magnitude has been stable for years.

**Utah (launch state, see [[Legal]] / [[Marketing]]):**
- Total nonfarm employment: **~1.79 million jobs** statewide as of December 2025 (Utah Dept. of Workforce Services).
- Restaurants and Other Eating Places specifically: **26,365 employees** as of December 2025 (Utah DWS industry brief) — note this is a narrow NAICS subcategory, not all of leisure/hospitality.
- Retail trade sector total headcount was not found this session (only year-over-year change data was available, showing the sector losing jobs through most of 2025) — worth pulling directly from Utah DWS's detailed industry tables as a follow-up.

**Not yet sized**: the other low-regulation expansion states identified in the [[Legal]] rollout roadmap — sizing should follow as that state-priority list solidifies.

Sources: [BLS CPS hourly-paid worker data](https://www.bls.gov/cps/cpsaat44.htm), [National Restaurant Association 2026 State of the Industry](https://restaurant.org/research-and-media/research/research-reports/state-of-the-industry/), [NRF Retail's Impact](https://nrf.com/research-insights/retails-impact), [American Staffing Association Q4 2025 data](https://americanstaffing.net/posts/2026/03/30/employment-and-sales-rebound-in-q4/), [Utah DWS December 2025 employment summary](https://www.utahbusiness.com/press-releases/2026/01/28/utah-employment-summary-december-2025-increased-state-economy/), [Utah DWS Leisure & Hospitality industry brief](https://jobs.utah.gov/wi/pubs/industrybriefs/leisure_mtnlnd.pdf)
