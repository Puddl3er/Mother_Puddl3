# Marketing

## Target segment (launch)
Hourly W-2 workforce businesses, broadly — not narrowly scoped to one vertical. Primary anchor verticals:
- Restaurants / food service
- Retail
- Staffing agencies / gig & temp labor (agencies placing hourly workers across client sites — instant pay is a differentiator agencies can use to attract workers)
- Broader SMB hourly workforce generally

**Roadmap (not launch scope):** independent contractors and salaried employees. Note: contractors are a materially different regulatory bucket than hourly W-2 workers — 1099 payments aren't "wages" and don't carry withholding obligations, so the EWA-vs-real-payroll distinction in [[Legal]] doesn't directly transfer to that segment and will need its own analysis when it's in scope.

## Competitive positioning: both fronts at once
Puddl3 positions against two categories simultaneously:
- **vs. traditional EWA apps** (DailyPay, Payactiv, Even, Branch): "real, tax-withheld payroll, not a wage advance" — no advance/loan framing, no repayment risk, because each payout is genuine final pay. Ties directly to the EWA-vs-payroll legal distinction in [[Legal]].
- **vs. legacy payroll providers** (Gusto, ADP, Rippling, Paychex): positioned as the modern payroll system of record that also happens to pay instantly — competing to be the primary payroll platform, not just a bolt-on to someone else's.

## Crypto/USDC/Stellar framing: hidden
Marketing message is simply "get paid the moment you clock out." USDC and Stellar do not surface anywhere in employer- or worker-facing marketing — mirrors the product decision (see [[Frontend-Backend]]) to hide crypto complexity entirely in the UX. No wallet/seed-phrase/crypto language in any marketing materials.

## Launch market: Utah
First launch state, chosen primarily for regulatory reasons (lightest pay-frequency/licensing burden — see [[Legal]]), not existing relationships. However, **specific pilot/target employer relationships in Utah are already in motion** — launch is not purely open-market prospecting from zero.

## Go-to-market motion: direct sales first, others follow
All three motions are the eventual model, but sequenced with direct sales prioritized first given limited startup resources:
1. **Direct sales to Utah employers (priority #1 now)** — leveraging existing in-motion relationships to land early customers/pilots directly.
2. **Channel partnerships** — staffing agencies, franchise groups, POS/T&A vendors who already have relationships with many hourly employers at once. Follows once direct sales proves the model.
3. **Worker-led bottom-up demand** — workers experience/hear about Puddl3 and pressure their employer to adopt it. Follows once there's a base of live employers/workers to generate this organically.

## "Zero Employer Cost" messaging: transparent, positioned as a benefit
Now that the employer payroll-wallet yield-share (see [[Revenue Models]]) is a confirmed revenue line, the decision is to market it openly rather than hide the mechanics: **"your payroll float earns interest, and we split it with you"** — turning the yield-share into an active selling point (the employer earns money, not just "avoids a fee") rather than fine-print detail. This is a stronger claim than plain "zero cost" and should be the lead message once legal confirms exactly how the yield-share can be structured/described (see the yield-bearing balances question in [[Legal]] — messaging should not get ahead of that legal confirmation).

## Open questions
- Exact yield-share split/number isn't finalized (see [[Revenue Models]]), so the "we split it with you" message can't include a specific percentage yet.
- Messaging must wait on legal confirmation of how the yield-bearing employer wallet can be structured/described before going out publicly (see [[Legal]]).
- Budget split across channel partnerships and worker-led motions (once direct sales phase matures) not yet decided.
